Research Details

Problem Statement

The behavior of shock waves propagating through conduits with porous walls is a complex phenomenon that has significant implications for industrial applications. The internal geometry of the porous walls plays a critical role in how shock waves interact with the conduit structure and dissipate energy.

Research Objectives

  • Investigate how porous wall geometry affects shock wave propagation
  • Analyze energy dissipation mechanisms in porous structures
  • Develop predictive models for shock wave behavior
  • Identify optimal wall configurations for desired shock wave characteristics
  • Compare experimental and computational results

Key Concepts

Shock Waves

Abrupt, violent changes in pressure and density that propagate through a medium at supersonic speeds.

Porous Media

Materials containing interconnected voids that allow fluid flow and can interact with pressure waves.

Wave Attenuation

The reduction in shock wave intensity as it travels through or interacts with porous structures.

Boundary Effects

How wall-fluid interactions influence shock wave propagation and energy transfer.

Research Significance

Understanding shock wave behavior in porous conduits has direct applications in:

  • Combustion Systems: Improved design of detonation engines and combustion chambers
  • Safety Engineering: Better protection systems against shock wave damage
  • Industrial Processes: Optimization of processes involving high-pressure waves
  • Material Science: Development of new porous materials with specific acoustic properties

Research Team

This research is being conducted at the Technion - Israel Institute of Technology, combining theoretical analysis, computational modeling, and experimental validation.
